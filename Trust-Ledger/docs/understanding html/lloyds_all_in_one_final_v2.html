Distributed ledger technology · explained in 90 seconds

Verify once.
Trust everywhere.

A KYC credential, issued once by Lloyds, cryptographically provable, never re-checked from scratch again. Follow the thread.

Scroll to follow the thread
The old way
Every bank starts from zero.

Same passport. Same utility bill. Same address proof — re-uploaded and re-checked every single time a customer applies somewhere new.

48hours, on average
The Lloyds way
Verified once. Remembered forever.

The credential is hashed, signed, and committed to a shared ledger — any connected institution can confirm it's real without touching the documents.

4.2minutes, on-chain
What stays private
Only proof travels. Never the documents.

Document hashes live on-chain. The actual identity documents and bank statements stay encrypted off-chain — no personally identifiable information ever touches the ledger.

Who can check it
Built for regulators, not against them.

A regulator runs its own observer node and sees the same verified history as everyone else — transparency without needing special access.

On the roadmap

Today it's one bank.
Tomorrow it's every bank.

The same credential Lloyds issues can be designed to extend across the industry — a shared trust network where any participating bank, NBFC, or fintech recognizes a customer's verified identity instantly.

01
One KYC, industry-wide
A customer verified at Lloyds never repeats the process at a partner bank, NBFC, or fintech on the network.
02
Shared fraud signal
A revoked or flagged credential is visible across every participating institution instantly — not siloed at one bank.
03
Faster onboarding, lower cost
Every institution on the network saves the cost of re-verifying identity that's already been cryptographically proven.

This isn't a faster form.
It's a trust layer the entire lending industry can finally share.
